# Environmental Constraints Report Style Context

This file is style guidance only. It is not project evidence, must not be
cited as a source, and must not be used to introduce facts into a current
project draft. Current project evidence, section policy, extent metadata,
source status, reviewer instructions, and validation guardrails always
override this style context.

This style context must not be cited.

Do not copy facts, place names, source results, alternative labels,
trail-specific assumptions, PEL-specific assumptions, conclusions, or agency
positions from any example report into Review Assist drafts. Use the patterns
below only to shape cautious, compact, review-candidate prose.

## General Tone

- Write in an objective environmental screening style.
- Keep paragraphs compact and editable.
- Refer to supporting tables and figures only when their IDs are supplied in
  the current request.
- State source limitations plainly.
- Avoid ranking, recommending, selecting, rejecting, approving, clearing, or
  making final jurisdictional, legal, agency, liability, or impact
  determinations.
- Avoid no-effect/no-impact language, permit required/not-required language,
  access/mitigation/construction commitments, cultural eligibility/effect
  determinations, contamination cleanup/liability conclusions, and demographic
  impact conclusions.

## Direct Project Evidence

- Use "within the project area," "within the submitted project features," or
  "within the comparison unit" only when the section policy and extent
  metadata show direct project evidence.
- Describe mapped intersections or source-backed summaries without implying
  field verification.

## Screening Buffer And Nearby Context

- Use "near," "in the vicinity," "within the screening buffer," or "within
  the context area" for screening or nearby context.
- Do not imply direct project impact unless direct evidence is supplied and
  the section policy allows that wording.
- Summarize relevant categories instead of dumping raw lists.

## Watershed And Regional Context

- Use watershed or subwatershed language only where the policy and evidence
  support watershed context.
- Discuss hydrologic setting or downstream context cautiously.
- County, regional, tract, or Census context must not be framed as direct
  project impact.

## Comparison Units

- Discuss alternatives or comparison units only when the policy requires or
  allows comparison-unit expansion.
- Describe source-backed differences without ranking alternatives or naming a
  preferred option.

## Wetlands And Waterbodies

- Briefly describe mapped wetlands, waterbodies, or hydrography evidence.
- Reference supplied table and figure IDs when useful.
- Include screening limitations: NWI and hydrography data support early
  screening and do not define final jurisdictional limits or field-delineated
  boundaries.

## Cultural Resources

- Distinguish public/coarse screening context from restricted or
  reviewer-supplied cultural-resource records.
- Do not imply public/coarse sources replace authorized MDAH, SHPO, Tribal, or
  agency cultural-resource review.
- Do not expose or infer restricted archaeological locations.

## Community Resources

- Use nearby/context language for community facilities.
- Do not imply service disruption, displacement, or direct impact from
  context-only evidence.
- Keep facility summaries bounded to the current evidence and source status.

## Utilities And Infrastructure

- Treat utility and infrastructure evidence as screening context unless the
  policy and evidence identify direct crossings.
- Do not determine conflicts, relocation needs, ownership, or service impacts.

## Regulated And Hazardous Facilities

- Distinguish facility registry context, cleanup or contamination context,
  discharge/permitted facilities, TRI, UST, Superfund, solid-waste, brownfield,
  and oil/gas well records where the evidence supports those distinctions.
- Do not imply every registry point is a hazardous waste site.
- Do not infer contamination extent, liability, cleanup obligation, or
  property impact.

## Manual Or Reviewer-Supplied Material

- State when reviewer-supplied documentation is required or pending.
- Do not draft unavailable P2/deferred/manual sections as if evidence exists.
